Phase-plan text for _risk_ and _mitigation_ is reproduced from each phase document's **§13 Risks & Mitigations**; this register adds the **category**, the **numeric score**, the **contingency** (what we do once the risk materialises), the **trigger / early-warning** signal and the **status**. When a phase plan changes, the phase plan wins and this register is regenerated from it.

## 1. How risks are scored

### 1.1 Likelihood (L) — probability within the risk's phase window (programme window for `PR-*`)

| Score | Label          | Probability | Phase-plan equivalent          |
| :---: | -------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------ |
|   5   | Almost certain | > 80 %      | — (programme calibration only) |
|   4   | Likely         | 50–80 %     | High                           |
|   3   | Possible       | 20–50 %     | Med                            |
|   2   | Unlikely       | 5–20 %      | Low                            |
|   1   | Rare           | < 5 %       | —                              |

### 1.2 Impact (I) — worst credible outcome if unmitigated

| Score | Label      | Money (USD, direct loss or unplanned spend) | Schedule                                                 | Customers                                      | Regulatory / reputational                                | Phase-plan equivalent |
| :---: | ---------- | ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------- |
|   5   | Severe     | > 1,000,000 or licence loss                 | Go-live 2027-12-10 or programme exit 2028-06-30 moves    | Widespread loss of funds or data               | Enforcement action, sanctions breach, licence suspension | Very high             |
|   4   | Major      | 250,000–1,000,000                           | Phase exit slips > 3 weeks or a market launch is delayed | A cohort's funds at risk or service down > 4 h | Regulatory finding or fine possible; partner suspension  | High                  |
|   3   | Moderate   | 50,000–250,000                              | A milestone slips 1–3 weeks                              | One corridor degraded; support spike           | Regulator or partner query                               | Med                   |
|   2   | Minor      | 10,000–50,000                               | Absorbed inside the phase (< 1 week)                     | Friction, no loss                              | Internal finding only                                    | Low                   |
|   1   | Negligible | < 10,000                                    | None                                                     | None                                           | None                                                     | —                     |

Assumptions behind the money bands: volumes ramp to the `schedule.json` target of 100,000 transfers/day by 2028-06-30; average remittance ticket USD 50–150; pre-funding capital of USD 50,000–150,000 (Phase 6) is returnable capital, not expense, and counts as loss only if unrecoverable.

### 1.3 Score and bands

**Score = L × I** (1–25). Phase plans use Low / Med / High; they are mapped Low → 2, Med → 3, High → 4 and "Very high" → 5. R4.3 ("Low (Phase 4) / Med (Phase 13)") takes the Phase 13 value, 3, because the register looks across the whole programme.

| Band         | Score | Required handling                                                                                                                                       |
| ------------ | :---: | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Critical** | 15–25 | Named owner reports weekly; escalated to the steering committee within 48 h of entering the band; contingency pre-approved (budget and decision rights) |
| **High**     | 10–14 | Reviewed at every fortnightly programme risk review; trigger wired to a dashboard or alert; contingency written and rehearsed where possible            |
| **Medium**   |  5–9  | Owned and reviewed in the phase's weekly stand-up; trigger documented                                                                                   |
| **Low**      |  1–4  | Accepted and monitored; reviewed at phase exit                                                                                                          |

## 2. Risk appetite statement

BI-Rem holds customer money and personal data across multiple jurisdictions. The board sets the following appetite; any risk whose plausible outcome exceeds a **tolerance** is escalated regardless of its score.

| Area                                | Appetite          | Tolerance (breach = immediate escalation)                                                                                                        |
| ----------------------------------- | ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Sanctions, AML and tipping-off      | **Zero**          | No transfer completed to a `CONFIRMED_MATCH`; 100 % of STR/SAR deadlines met; no customer-facing text that discloses an investigation            |
| Customer funds and ledger integrity | **Zero**          | Trial balance zero per currency every night; `birem_ledger_unbalanced_total = 0`; reconciliation breaks resolved or explained within 24 h        |
| Card data (Phase 13)                | **Zero**          | No PAN in BI-Rem logs, databases or object storage (vault-only architecture)                                                                     |
| Personal data protection            | **Very low**      | No Restricted data in logs or analytics; breaches notified within statutory windows (72 h for NDPA, Kenya DPA and GDPR-aligned regimes)          |
| Fraud losses                        | **Low**           | Confirmed fraud loss ≤ 0.05 % of weekly transferred value (assumption; recalibrated with Phase 8 data)                                           |
| Service availability                | **Low**           | 99.9 % monthly API availability from Phase 12; rollout freezes when < 25 % of the error budget remains                                           |
| Licensing timeline                  | **Moderate**      | We accept launching on a partner's licence with caps and a reduced country count; we never operate a corridor without a documented licence basis |
| Delivery schedule                   | **Moderate**      | One week of slip per phase is absorbed; > 3 days on the critical chain (Phase 4 → 5 → 6 → 8 → 10 → 12) is escalated                              |
| Budget                              | **Moderate**      | Each phase stays within its high estimate; cumulative spend above the midpoint + 10 % is escalated                                               |
| Technology and vendors              | **Moderate–high** | Recent framework majors and managed vendors (Neon, Fly.io, Vercel, Upstash, ClickHouse Cloud) are accepted provided an exit path is documented   |
| Market and product experiments      | **High**          | New corridors, segments and channels are trialled behind flags with caps; failure of an experiment is acceptable, harm to customers is not       |

## 5. Top-10 risks

Ranked by score, then by proximity of the trigger date and by how many phases the risk spans. These ten are reported to every monthly steering committee.

1. **PR-01 — Licensing and no-objection delays (score 20, Regulatory, Phases 6/12/13).** Thirteen regulators stand between the plan and its country targets; none of their timelines is within our control and several (CBN IMTO, SARB/ADLA, COBAC) run 6–12 months. The register treats this as the single largest risk because both hard business outcomes — GA in ≥ 3 markets on 2027-12-10 and 18 live countries by M13.12 (2028-06-16) — depend on it. The mitigation is structural, not procedural: every corridor carries a `licenceBasis` (own licence, sponsor bank, partner EMI, aggregator), so a missing own licence changes _how_ we launch, not _whether_. Early warning is the licensing matrix: an application without regulator acknowledgement eight weeks after filing turns the row red. Decision needed now: file NG (CBN IMTO) and ZA (ADLA partner) applications in 2027-Q3/Q4 rather than in Phase 13 W2–W6 as the phase plan assumes.
2. **PR-23 — Critical-path compression (16, Operational).** Phases 4 → 5 → 6 → 8 → 10 → 12 carry zero calendar float and Phase 5 overlaps Phase 4 by two weeks ([critical path](./01-gantt-chart.md#4-critical-path)). Any slip moves go-live one for one. Contingency is per-phase scope ladders already written into the plans (R5.11, R6.7, R8.8, R10.4); the trigger is any critical-chain milestone > 3 days late.
3. **R6.1 — COBAC sponsor bank not signed by W6 (16, Regulatory).** Without a sponsor bank / payment-institution arrangement in Cameroon there is no real CEMAC outbound money movement, which is the anchor of the Phase 6 success criteria and of the Phase 12 pilot (CM is the anchor market, R12.15). The phase can ship dark and friends-and-family can run under the partner EMI's licence with caps; the trigger is an unsigned term sheet on 2027-06-25 (W4). Programme action: the bank short-list must be in negotiation by Phase 4 (see [external dependencies](./03-dependency-map.md#5-external-dependencies)).
4. **PR-04 — Currency controls in Angola and CEMAC (16, Regulatory).** BNA allocation rules and BEAC exchange regulation can block outbound flows or demand documentary evidence per transfer. The product stays inbound-only for AO/MZ in Phase 6 and keeps documentary thresholds as Settings; the early warning is a new BNA or BEAC circular or a declaration rejected by the sponsor bank.
5. **PR-05 — Fraud losses (16, Financial).** Domestic transfers go live on 2027-05-28 while full AML/fraud enforcement only arrives with Phase 8 on 2027-09-17; agent cash-in (Phase 9) and card funding (Phase 13) add new vectors. The interim controls (R5.9) and crime/fidelity insurance before the first PILOT are the bridge; the tolerance is 0.05 % of weekly value.
6. **R8.1 — Sanctions matcher false positives on African and Arabic names (16, Technical).** If the in-house fuzzy matcher's false-positive rate is too high, the SANCTIONS queue floods, SLAs break and legitimate customers are held. Golden set calibration (recall ≥ 99 %, FPR ≤ 3 %) and a shadow period in Phase 8 W2–W3 are the mitigation; capacity is planned at 60 alerts per analyst per day.
7. **PR-10 — Sanctions list changes and geopolitical events (16, Regulatory).** A missed designation is a strict-liability breach. Daily ingestion with delta re-screening and staleness alerts (Phase 8) must be live before cross-border volume ramps; the trigger is a `WatchlistStale` alert or a designation affecting a live corridor or partner bank.
8. **PR-03 — FX volatility and liquidity (16, Market).** NGN, AOA, MZN and GHS have all shown double-digit moves; nostros across six currencies can run dry at month-end peaks (R6.4). Guard rails, short lock windows, position limits and forecast-driven rebalancing (Phase 7) contain it; the trigger is a daily mid move > 3 % on a live pair or treasury runway < 12 h.
9. **PR-12 — Pentest findings accumulate (16, Security).** About ten external tests run across the programme, culminating in the Phase 12 test that gates board #1 (R12.1, R12.2). Recurring finding classes are the early warning; the contingency is a compensating control with CTO risk acceptance agreed by the vendor, never a silent waiver.
10. **PR-02 — Provider API instability (16, Provider).** The programme scales to 50 providers whose sandboxes diverge from production (R4.2) and some of which lack idempotency (R4.4). Conformance suites, simulators, circuit breakers and two routes per corridor are the mitigation; a provider with more than three incidents a month is placed on a remediation plan or demoted to secondary route.

**Watch list (score 15, just below the top ten):** PR-06 key-person dependency (single DevOps/SRE and compliance analyst), PR-08 data residency, PR-18 sponsor-bank de-risking. **Highest single impact:** R9.6 tenant isolation (impact 5, likelihood 2).

## 7. Review cadence and governance

| Forum                            | Frequency                                              | Chair                       | Scope                                                          | Output                                                   |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hypercare stand-up               | Daily 08:00 WAT during Phase 12 and each Phase 13 wave | Incident commander on shift | Triggers for risks tagged to the launching market              | Launch report entry; new issues raised                   |

| Programme risk review            | Fortnightly                                            | Program Manager             | Every risk scoring ≥ 10, all `PR-*`, any fired trigger         | Regenerated register and CSV; escalations                |
| Steering committee               | Monthly and at each phase gate                         | Executive sponsor           | Top-10, Critical band, appetite breaches, budget risks (PR-13) | Decisions on contingencies, re-baselines, accepted risks |
| Go/no-go boards #1–#3 (Phase 12) | Per ladder step                                        | CEO / COO                   | Register snapshot is a mandatory input of the readiness pack   | Conditions attached to GO decisions                      |
| Compliance committee             | Monthly                                                | MLRO                        | Regulatory risks, PR-01/04/10/16                               | Licensing-matrix status; regulator correspondence        |

**Status lifecycle.** `Open` (identified, mitigations planned) → `Mitigating` (mitigation in progress) → `Triggered` (early-warning fired; contingency invoked and an issue opened) → `Closed` (risk window passed or eliminated), or `Accepted` (steering committee accepts residual risk, recorded with date) or `Transferred` (to a later phase, insurer or partner). Phase risks are closed or transferred at each phase exit review; a risk transferred to a later phase keeps its id.

**Escalation rules.**

- A risk entering the **Critical** band is reported to the steering committee within 48 h.
- A fired trigger requires owner action within one business day and an entry in the weekly status report.
- Any **Regulatory** or **Security** risk with impact 5 goes the same day to the CEO and, respectively, the MLRO or the security engineer and CTO.
- Any breach of an appetite tolerance (section 2) is escalated immediately, independent of the risk's score.

**Maintenance.** The register is regenerated from the phase plans' §13 tables and the programme-risk list; the CSV is the import format for the PM tool and the Phase 12 `go_live_gates` readiness packs. New risks raised mid-phase are added to the phase plan first (next free `R<phase>.<n>`) so that ids stay stable.
